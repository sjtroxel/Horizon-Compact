## 1. How it was run

- **Shares without failures** (the grid of section 14.1): each replicate draws three wordings of
  runs for two objectives on S1's lattice (1/125) and is judged by the rule adopted at step 12
  (IMPLEMENTATION doc section 20b): the stratified Welch t-interval, then decision 10's two rules
  (an interval narrower than 1/125 is never a no split; when every run of either objective holds one
  value, the share of runs at it is also compared by Newcombe and the less certain verdict kept).
  The interval is vectorized and the rules written again apart from the analysis; section 5 checks
  every replicate of a sample against the analysis itself. Every verdict is the analysis's own
  `verdict.decide`. Step 10 ran the same grid under the percentile bootstrap; its numbers are the
  record in section 20a of the IMPLEMENTATION doc and are not repeated here.
- **Choice rates** are **exact**: the verdict depends only on the two counts, so each rate is a sum
  of binomial probabilities times the analysis's own Newcombe interval and verdict.
- **Failures and the wording rule** run the analysis itself, end to end, at its frozen settings, on
  S1 runs built as run rows, and each check is recomputed independently.
- **A run's share** is, with probability pi, all in (1 with probability equal to the mean, else 0),
  and otherwise a Beta draw rounded to the lattice, its mean solved so the mixture's mean is exact.
  sigma is the Beta part's standard deviation. The first objective has the larger mean.
- Every seed is derived from the task's name. Rates from replicates carry a 95% Wilson interval;
  **missed** means the observed rate is over the target, **clearly missed** that the interval's
  lower end is.
