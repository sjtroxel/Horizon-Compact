### 1. What in this protocol would let a result be bent, a claim overstated, or a verdict reached for a reason other than the data?

*   **Where:** Section 12.4, sentences 1 and 5.
*   **Problem:** The protocol says candidates are taken "in order of first disclosure," but simultaneously states "preferred, a manufacturer of comparable size." This undefined preference allows the author to subjectively skip an older, unliked case by claiming a newer one fits the "preferred" size better.
*   **Seriousness:** Would change a verdict (by altering which real cases are run).
*   **Suggested change:** Define "comparable size" precisely (e.g., revenue bounds) and specify it as a hard filter applied *before* strict date ordering.

**2. The author can overrule the independent reader**
*   **Where:** Section 12.4, sentence 6 ("Every rejection on criterion 3, 4 or 5...").
*   **Problem:** If the independent reader disagrees with a candidate's rejection, the disagreement "is resolved by him" (the author). This gives the author ultimate veto power, rendering the independent check toothless against motivated inclusions/exclusions.
*   **Seriousness:** Would let a result be bent.
*   **Suggested change:** Require that any disagreement from the independent reader automatically resolves to the opposite of the author's initial judgment, or drops the candidate entirely.

**3. Total discretion on scaling-check failures**
*   **Where:** Section 12.6, bullet 6 ("Scaling"), final sentence.
*   **Problem:** If a case fails the pay-per-head rounding check, it is "decided by him with the reason logged." This grants the author total discretion to either drop the case or manually patch figures after seeing how the real-world data scaled.
*   **Seriousness:** Would let a result be bent.
*   **Suggested change:** State the exact mechanical consequence of a failed check (e.g., "the case fails criterion 4 and is replaced by the next eligible candidate").

**4. Opaque "extraction model"**
*   **Where:** Section 12.6, bullet 1 ("Only documents dated..."), last sentence.
*   **Problem:** The protocol relies on an "extraction model" to parse SEC filings into the dossier but never names the model, its configuration, or the prompt it uses, leaving a critical data-processing step completely unfrozen.
